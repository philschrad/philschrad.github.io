Why the jolt happens

Lateral acceleration is v²κ. If the circle starts straight after the straight, κ jumps from 0 to 1/R in zero distance. The lateral acceleration then jumps too, so jerk is in principle unbounded. A passenger feels a lurch, and the strap overshoots and swings.

What the clothoid does

Along an Euler spiral, curvature grows linearly with arc length: κ(s) = s/(R·Ls). Lateral acceleration then ramps up at a constant jerk of v³/(R·Ls). The circle also has to move inward, by a shift of about Ls²/(24R). The dashed ghost shows it.

Link to minimax spirality

Track design usually fits a single spiral like this. Kaya, Noakes & Schrader (ESAIM: COCV 2026) show that a curve of fixed length that minimises the peak |dκ/ds| is generally a C² chain of several spirals, or of spirals, arcs and lines when |κ| is bounded.

Model is simplified: planar track, no cant (superelevation), so all lateral acceleration is uncompensated. The strap is a damped pendulum, 1 m long with damping ratio 0.1, driven by v²κ. It is there to show the effect, not to give real comfort figures.
